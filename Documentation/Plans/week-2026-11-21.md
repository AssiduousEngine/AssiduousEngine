## The sequencer, Q3 and Q4 (2026-11-21)

- **A view from another camera starts its history fresh** (acbb7a2e9): when a scene's camera pick
  moved to another camera, the view kept its history slot and blended the old camera's TAA,
  reflections, GI and exposure into the first frames. A history slot remembers the camera it was
  drawn from; another is a cut.
- **Camera cuts** (092662268): a track makes its bound camera the scene's view while a section
  runs, through claims on the view (runtime state, never saved): the play begun last wins, letting
  go hands the view back. Plays gained a start order for it.
- **Fades** (3f87bc20a): a colour mixed over the whole view, the tonemap's last step, so the HUD and
  subtitles draw over it; a claim on the scene's post-process system, won as the camera is.
- **Skeletal clips** (148ef0fb5): sections on an actor's pose overlay (PoseOverlay::DriveOneShot),
  so a seek lands on the frame while the graph underneath keeps running.
- **Sounds** (3f78d54e2): sections in the scene's audio group, spatial at the actor or flat; a voice
  starts part way into a section entered late and is moved back when it drifts, so it stays on the
  timeline through seeks and speed changes. Plays tell runners when they pause.
- **The editor** (b7bbbef1b, bcf87f202, dfe4246c5, 9de8c3cd8, 0779c2b79, bf6a68427, 3d5249e12): the
  sequence document with its own history and a restoring preview; section rows in the toolkit's
  dopesheet (bars moved and trimmed as one gesture); each track kind saying how its keys and
  sections are edited, so a kind a plugin adds edits with no list to extend; the dopesheet and
  curve view over the document; the kinds' fields in the inspector; the panel under the scene's
  viewport (Cast, Track, Section, markers and holds, its own undo) and a double click that adds a
  section.
- **MCP** (74f0019f6): `sequence_read`, `sequence_edit` (a list of edits written once, or nothing)
  and `sequence_preview` on the editor host.
- **A track's runner may hold the play** (bda7bfc82), for a line of dialogue on the timeline that
  waits to be read. A per-scene start order is in the backlog to revisit (074260977).
- The spec records Q3 and Q4 built (804550981, 247608c79), with what differs from the plan: sections
  are dopesheet rows, the panel is a pane beside the animation panel, preview cuts and the panel's
  sound on play are not built. The commits were rewritten to drop shorthand namespace aliases
  (`seq`, `propanim`), and the specs cite the new hashes (465f5b497).

- **The online spec refreshed** (Specs/online.md, still proposed): checked against today's code

  and the user's rulings: the Steam SDK lives outside the tree and must be provided when the plugin
  is enabled, the official templates carry no Steam and a Steam game exports natively, the game's

  editor uses the enabled backend. A new decision and phase add outside dependencies to the plugin
  API (declared in the manifest, located like the Android SDK, checked at enable, build, play and
  export, deploy files from inside them).
- **RHI handles proposed** (Specs/rhi-handles.md): the user asked whether handles would make the
  RHI better or safer. Typed generational handles for every resource other objects or commands
  refer to, over one handle and pool in Core; the device owns the tables and frees a destroyed
  resource once the submissions that used it complete, replacing GpuRetireQueue and the WaitIdle
  fallbacks; caches key on handles. A survey found physics joints and characters and navigation
  agents reusing slots without a generation; they move onto the Core handle last.
- **RHI handles H0: Core's handle and pool** (branch `rhi-handles`, Specs/rhi-handles.md):
  `Handle<Tag>` and `HandlePool<Tag, T>` in a new `:handle_pool` partition. A slot's count is bumped
  when it is freed, so every copy of a handle goes stale; the generation's top 8 bits are the pool's
  id, from a process-wide table in Core's implementation unit keyed on the signature type id (one
  per process in the shared build), so another pool's handle fails; a count that would wrap retires
  the slot; values sit in fixed pages published once, so lookups on other threads are safe while
  the owner allocates. Core.Tests: eight cases (stale copies, reuse, another pool, ids per type and
  freed with the pool, wrap, growth and fullness, ForEach and destruction, the owner's allocator, a
  reader thread during growth). The spec's threads gotcha now says what the pool guarantees. Lanes:
  clang 211/211, build checked apart.
- **RHI handles H1: buffers by handle, end to end** (branch `rhi-handles`): the RHI names buffers
  by `BufferHandle`; what was a buffer's own method or field is a device call (`Describe`, `Map`,
  `Unmap`, `FlushRange`). A new `:lifetime` partition holds `SubmissionTracker` and
  `ResourceTable`: a destroyed buffer's handle is stale at once, and the buffer waits until every
  recording begun before the destroy has been submitted or discarded and those submissions have
  completed (the user's ruling on command buffers recorded before a destroy and submitted after;
  the spec's Decision 4 updated). Vulkan's queues and transfer batches signal a per-queue timeline
  semaphore on every submit, Direct3D 12's a per-queue fence (not compiled here: the Windows build
  checks it), Null's submissions complete as made, WebGPU releases at once (wgpu keeps what
  recorded and submitted work uses). Pools begin, end and discard recordings. Validation labels
  buffers by handle and reports a stale use with the destroyed buffer's label (the last 1024 kept).
  About 130 caller files converted, partly by scripts over the compiler's errors. `Handle` gained an
  explicit bool, as a pointer tested. Tests: the tracker's rules (submissions before a retire,
  recorded-before-submitted-after, discards, per queue, freed at teardown), the Null device's
  stale handles, recording-held frees and another device's handles, validation's labelled
  report, and on Vulkan a buffer destroyed after commands naming it were recorded living until they
  ran (a mutant that ignores open recordings fails both). Lanes: clang 211/211, build checked apart.
- **RHI handles H2: textures, views and samplers by handle** (branch `rhi-handles`): the RHI names
  them by `TextureHandle`, `TextureViewHandle` and `SamplerHandle`, retired and freed as buffers
  are. What callers read off the objects is a device call: `Describe` for each, and `TextureOf` for
  a view's texture. Each backend's tables are one struct (`VkTables`, `DxTables`, `WebGpuTables`)
  that encoders, bind groups and transfer batches hold, looking any handle up with one `LookUp`. A
  swap chain's images and views are in the tables while it holds them and taken back out (not
  retired) when it rebuilds or goes; WebGPU gives every acquired image handles of its own. A view
  outlives its texture's destroy and then names a stale texture; Direct3D 12's view keeps its own
  reference to the resource instead of a pointer to the texture (not compiled here). Validation's
  per-type records (live handles, labels, the last 1024 destroyed) cover all four types and name
  a stale view after its texture. `TextureView::uniqueId` and `Texture::initialState` are gone:
  the sprite, decal, particle and terrain caches key on view handles, which never repeat, and the
  render graph keeps a texture's mip and layer counts on its resource. About 200 caller files
  converted, partly by a script over the compiler's errors. Tests: the Null device's stale
  textures, views and samplers, a view outliving its texture, a recording holding a texture to its
  submit, swap chain image handles going with it, another device's texture refused; validation's
  labelled stale view; on Vulkan a texture and view destroyed after commands naming them were
  recorded living until they ran (a mutant that frees at once fails it). Lanes: clang 211/211,
  build checked apart.
